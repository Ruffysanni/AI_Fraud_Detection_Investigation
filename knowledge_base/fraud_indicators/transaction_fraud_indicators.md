# Transaction Fraud Indicators

**Document type:** Illustrative fraud investigation guidance  
**Document ID:** FI-001  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Prototype knowledge-base document

## 1. Purpose

This document describes transaction patterns that may warrant further investigation during financial fraud monitoring. These indicators are intended to support preliminary risk assessment and do not independently establish that fraudulent activity has occurred.

## 2. Unusual Transaction Amounts

Transactions involving amounts significantly different from an account's established activity may require further examination.

Potential indicators:
- A transaction substantially larger than the account's historical transaction amounts.
- Multiple unusually large transactions occurring within a short period.
- A sudden increase in transaction value compared with previous activity.

Investigation considerations:
- Compare the transaction amount with the account's historical activity.
- Examine whether similar transactions have occurred previously.
- Consider the transaction type and available account context.

## 3. Rapid Movement of Funds

Rapid movement of funds may warrant investigation, particularly where money is transferred shortly after being received.

Potential indicators:
- Funds received and transferred out shortly afterwards.
- Several outgoing transfers within a short period.
- Repeated movement of funds between multiple accounts.

Investigation considerations:
- Establish the sequence and timing of transactions.
- Identify related transactions where records are available.
- Examine whether the movement is consistent with established account activity.

## 4. Unusual Balance Changes

Unexpected changes in account balances may be relevant to a fraud investigation.

Potential indicators:
- A substantial reduction in the origin account balance following a transaction.
- A sudden increase in a destination account balance.
- A transaction that reduces an account balance to zero.

Investigation considerations:
- Compare balances before and after the transaction.
- Verify that the recorded transaction amount is consistent with the balance changes.
- Examine other transactions around the same period.

## 5. Repeated or High-Frequency Transactions

Unusually frequent transactions may indicate activity requiring additional review.

Potential indicators:
- Multiple transfers occurring within a short period.
- Repeated transactions involving the same destination account.
- A sudden increase in transaction frequency.

Investigation considerations:
- Establish the number and frequency of transactions.
- Compare activity with the account's historical pattern.
- Identify related transaction records.

## 6. Transaction Type Considerations

Certain transaction types may require additional scrutiny depending on their context.

Examples include:
- TRANSFER: Examine the source, destination, amount and timing.
- CASH_OUT: Examine the withdrawal amount and preceding account activity.
- CASH_IN: Examine the source and subsequent movement of funds.

No transaction type should automatically be classified as fraudulent without supporting evidence.

## 7. Investigation Limitations

The presence of one or more indicators does not prove fraud.

Investigators should:
- Consider multiple sources of evidence.
- Distinguish observed facts from assumptions.
- Consider legitimate explanations for unusual activity.
- Document uncertainty and missing information.
- Recommend further review where evidence is insufficient.

## 8. Disclaimer

This is illustrative guidance created for a fraud detection prototype. It is not an official financial institution policy, regulatory standard or legal determination.
